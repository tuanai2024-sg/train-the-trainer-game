                How to read the numbers, and the assumptions
                Value per year
                The cost saved, gross margin gained or carrying cost avoided each year once the optimizer's plan runs at full scale, compared with today's method. It is before tax and before removing overlap with other problems that share the same cost.
                How it is converted into money
                Addressable cost pool × scope × improvement found by the optimizer on sample data × conversion and realization factor. The factor removes what is not kept in real operations: real-world constraints, people not following the plan, data that differs from the sample.
                Cash released
                For inventory and receivables problems: money taken out of working capital once. The value per year is the cost of holding that money, at 14% a year (10% cost of capital, 4% storage and obsolescence).
                Bad-year loss avoided
                For risk problems: the difference between the two options in a bad year (1 in 10 scenarios), not a yearly profit. In a normal year the lower-risk option can cost slightly more.

                Assumptions

                • Vertex Group is a simulated company with consolidated revenue of 60,000 billion VND: Vertex Logistics 12,000 billion, Vertex Retail 20,000 billion, Vertex Consumer 16,000 billion, Vertex Industrial 12,000 billion. Every number, trial-run data set and decision is illustrative.
                • Trial-run data covers one sample region, product group or week, generated once for each problem; the algorithms really run in your browser.
                • Values exclude software costs; this page asks which plan is better for the business, not how much to spend on tools.
                • Value ramps up over about 9 months after full rollout. Problems that share the same cost (for example delivery routes and driver zones) cannot simply be added together.
